Composite Front Wing FEA (Formula SAE-style load cases)

Laminate design and finite-element analysis of a carbon-fibre race-car front wing: the final layup carries the worst-case 4.1 g bump load at 74% of its allowable stress and weighs about 1.17 kg, 77% lighter than the first design.

My role: a University of Auckland course project using Formula SAE-style loads (not Formula SAE team work). I designed the laminate, built the Abaqus shell model, applied the three load cases, ran the ply-level stress checks and iterated the layup for mass.

The governing failure mode is transverse compression in the unidirectional plies next to the rod-end attachments under the bump case, remaining 26% below the chosen allowable stress, which includes a safety factor of 1.5.

How I modelled it

  • Abaqus/Standard S4R shell model with one composite layup; mesh refined to 3 mm at the eight rod-end attachments, 5 mm at the leading and trailing edges and 10 mm elsewhere.
  • Mesh check under the bump case: peak displacement changed by less than 0.3% between a coarse mesh of about 6,000 elements and the final 54,998-element mesh.
  • Rod-ends modelled as spherical joints through kinematic couplings (translations fixed, rotations free).
  • Ply-level maximum-stress check against fatigue allowables for the working cases and ultimate allowables ÷ 1.5 for the bump case.
  • Honeycomb core considered and rejected: the double-curved surface complicates core machining and bonding, and the adhesive films alone would add about 400 g/m².

Design iteration

  • Initial layup: 2.45 mm thick, about 3,750 g/m², under 2% utilisation in every case, so heavily over-designed.
  • Final layup: symmetric, balanced [woven 0° / UD ±45° / UD 0°]s, eight plies, 0.89 mm thick, 850 g/m².
  • Result: 77% lighter, all three load cases still met, and working-load deflection under 0.4 mm against the 10 mm limit.

Next steps: local ply drop-offs in the low-stress mid-span to save more mass, and coupon tests of the selected laminate.
