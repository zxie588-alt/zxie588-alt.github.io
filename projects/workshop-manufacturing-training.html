University Workshop Training (laser cutting, CNC, 3D printing)

Undergraduate workshop training in three processes: I turned my own AutoCAD DXF profile into a laser-cut and engraved metal plate, and trained in CNC milling and 3D printing.

My role: Trainee. I prepared the DXF geometry, checked the cut part's contour quality and cleaned it up, worked through a basic 3D printing workflow, and practised or observed CNC milling setup and safety.

What I practised

  • Prepared 2D vector geometry in AutoCAD and exported it as DXF for laser cutting and engraving.
  • Checked the laser-cut part against the drawing for contour quality, then cleaned it up.
  • Practised or observed CNC milling: workholding, spindle and tool motion, cutting fluid, chips and machine safety.
  • Reviewed machined gear and shaft examples to see how CAD geometry becomes real manufactured features.
  • Worked through a basic 3D printing workflow, from digital geometry to build orientation and post-processing.

Why it matters

  • Design geometry has to survive real machines, materials and setups.
  • Better judgement on sharp corners, clearances, part orientation, tool access and post-processing.
  • Easier, more practical conversations with technicians, machinists and fabricators.

In a New Zealand workplace I would expect a site induction and WorkSafe-aligned procedures before any hands-on work.

From drawing to part

The DXF profile and the plate cut from it, milling and machined examples, and two hand-drawing exercises.

Where I have used it since

  • Energy-harvesting backpack, University of Auckland: laser-cut acrylic plates, 3D-printed parts and a welded generator support for the suspended-load platform I designed and built.
  • Outdoor IoT sensor enclosure: manufacturability choices for screw bosses, the gasket and lid details.
